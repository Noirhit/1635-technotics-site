Sheet 01 of 05 The crew

Twenty-five students.
One shop. One deadline.

There is no tryout and no prerequisite. Show up at the shop, pick a subteam, and by February you will have built something that moves.

§ 01 — Why we do it

The robot is the excuse. The engineers are the point.

Newtown High School sits in Elmhurst, one of the most linguistically diverse neighbourhoods on earth. Our roster reflects it exactly.

Each January FIRST releases the season's game. Six weeks later a 120-pound robot has to pass inspection. In between, students learn mechanical design, electrical systems, software and project management the only way that really works — with something real on the line and a clock running.

Most of them arrive knowing nothing. That is the intended starting point. The seniors running our mill were freshmen who could not read a caliper three years ago.

§ 03 — Build season

Kickoff crate to competition floor in about 45 days.

The same six weeks every team on earth gets. Scroll the rail sideways.

01 Jan · Kickoff

Read the rules. Argue strategy.

Whole team in one room with whiteboards, the kit-of-parts and pizza. We leave with a strategy and three rough concepts.

02 Prototype

Wood, zip-ties, bad ideas.

Mechanisms in plywood and extrusion. The two that survive become real subsystems; the rest teach us something.

03 CAD lock

Design the whole machine.

Prototypes become a full assembly — frame, gearboxes, intake and wiring laid out on one model before a chip is cut.

04 Manufacture

CNC, mill, print, repeat.

Parts come off the mill and the printers. Programming starts on a chassis-only practice base.

05 Integrate

Wire it. Code it. Break it.

Subsystems mounted, electronics flashed, autonomous run over and over against a taped field until it stops surprising us.

06 Compete

Load the truck.

Final tuning, driver practice, binder finished — then an early bus to the Armory.
