// CHAPTER 01 · WHO WE ARE

About us.

The students, mentors and machines that make 1635 Technotics what it is. A robotics team that happens to be a public high school.

§ 01 — MISSION

Newtown High School sits in Elmhurst — a neighborhood where the students collectively speak dozens of languages. Every winter a chunk of them spend their nights over a CAD model, a soldering iron, and a half-built drivetrain.

We're the Newtown Technotics — FRC Team 1635. Each January, FIRST releases the season's game; six weeks later a 120-pound robot rolls off our shop floor. In between is the part the team is really about: learning mechanical engineering, electrical, software and project management against a deadline that does not move.

Since our rookie year in 2005 we've competed every single season — most recently at the NYC Regional inside the Armory, where you'll find us in the stands holding three-foot-tall red 16 and 35 and never quite sitting down.

§ 03 — THE SIX-WEEK BUILD

From kickoff crate to competition floor in about 45 days.

FRC is a sprint with a fixed deadline. Each January, FIRST releases the year's game; teams worldwide get the same kit and the same calendar. Here's how 1635 runs it.

  1. W01JAN · KICKOFF

    Read the rules. Argue strategy.

    The whole team in one room with whiteboards, the kit-of-parts, and pizza. We leave with a strategy and a few rough concepts.

  2. W02PROTOTYPE

    Wood, zip-ties, bad ideas.

    Mechanism prototypes in plywood and extrusion. The ones that survive become the robot's real subsystems.

  3. W03CAD LOCK

    Design the whole machine.

    Prototypes become a full CAD assembly — frame, gearboxes, intake and wiring laid out on one model.

  4. W04MANUFACTURE

    CNC, mill, print, repeat.

    Parts come off the mill and the 3D printers. Programming starts on a chassis-only practice base.

  5. W05INTEGRATE

    Wire it. Code it. Test it.

    Subsystems mounted, electronics flashed, autonomous routines tested over and over against a taped field.

  6. W06COMPETE

    Load the truck.

    Final tuning, driver practice, paperwork in the binder — then an early bus to the Armory.
